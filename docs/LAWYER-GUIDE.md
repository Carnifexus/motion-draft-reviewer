# Lawyer Guide: Conduct a motion draft review

## 1. Choose the decision you need

Ask for a particular review, not a global rewrite. For example: “Review this version for whether the record supports the requested order. Prioritize defects that could change what the court grants. Do not edit, send, or file anything.” If the motion is still early, ask for the missing proof and plausible narrower relief. If it's late, give the reviewer the exact versions of the motion and proposed order you expect to use.

## 2. Assemble a small packet

Include the motion, its exhibits or searchable excerpts, the proposed order, the governing primary authority, and any briefing, scheduling, or standing order the motion depends on. Supply the opponent's actual response when it exists. Identify which record items are filed, stipulated, disputed, or not yet in the record. Give each source a stable ID so you can relocate a finding after pagination shifts.

Say which court and jurisdiction apply and what posture the motion is in. When a timeline matters, add a one-page chronology with dates and source pinpoints. Include known source defects, such as a witness who lacks personal knowledge or an attachment whose origin has not been established. Don't load the entire file just in case. Use the firm's approved workspace and follow its data-handling rules before inserting confidential or restricted material.

## 3. Run the skill

Open [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) and give it to your approved AI tool as instructions. Provide the packet in that same approved workspace. For a first run, use the [fictional packet](../examples/FICTIONAL-INPUT.md) and compare the result to the [illustrative output](../examples/FICTIONAL-OUTPUT.md).

A useful prompt is:

> Review the attached draft and proposed order as of the date shown. Work request by request. Rely on the supplied record. Do not assume omitted materials are absent from the case. Label the strongest actual opposition and any additional hypothetical argument. Give me a bottom line and a prefiling checklist. Do not modify or transmit anything.

If you authorize legal research, define the scope and use current primary sources or firm-approved research tools for it. Confirm all governing texts, citations, and procedural deadlines independently before you rely on them.

## 4. Work through the report

Read the bottom line first, but open the source behind any finding that could change what you file. For every blocking item, decide whether to supply proof, revise a statement, narrow relief, seek a procedural cure, or omit the request. Do not use an AI-generated assertion to bridge a gap in the record. If the critique relies on an inference, make sure you agree it is a reasonable one.

For opposition, ask whether the strongest adverse fact has been put fairly. If the opponent has not responded, use the hypothetical analysis to prepare for hearing, not as evidence of anyone's position. If you decide to leave a weakness in the motion, record why so it is not inadvertently treated as fixed.

## 5. Verify the final version yourself

Make a final comparison of the motion, proposed order, and retained record pinpoints. Check the law, local rules, judge's orders, signing and service requirements, deadlines, and any court-specific sealing or redaction requirements. Inspect the actual final files, not just the drafts. Preserve the review privately under your firm's retention rules. This workflow is not legal advice to clients, a filing service, or a guarantee of privilege or outcome.
